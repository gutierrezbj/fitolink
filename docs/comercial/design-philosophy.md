# Botanical Cartography

## Philosophy

A visual register where the rigour of cartographic catalogues meets the patient attention of botanical plates. Information is not announced — it is **inventoried**. Each item is a specimen entered into a notebook by hand, dated, numbered, indexed with the discipline of a 19th-century field naturalist who has chosen to study a discipline that did not exist yet: precision farming.

The page does not seduce. It serves. It earns its place on a wall, in a glove compartment, on the kitchen table next to the boletines of the local agricultural authority. The agricultor recognises this typographic register — it is the register of the SIGPAC map, the cuaderno de campo, the Ministry pamphlet. The work claims authority by **resembling the trustworthy ephemera** the reader has handled all their life, then quietly transcends it through master-level execution.

## Visual expression

**Space and form.** A4 portrait, treated as an entomologist's plate. Generous outer margins. A spine of vertical breathing room that lets each section settle. Hairlines do the structural work — never boxes, never shadow, never the flat blocks of modern infographics. Every separator is the width of a single line of ink drawn by a meticulous hand.

**Color and material.** A palette of paper, parchment, ink, ochre rule and a single saturated accent in terra-oxide. The accent appears like a hand-stamped seal — sparingly, on numerals and on the cardinal markers of the document, never on the body. Whites are not white. Greens are not green. Everything tints toward the warm earth of a manuscript that has survived a season in a leather portfolio.

**Scale and rhythm.** Roman numerals carry the weight of order. Display serif holds the headlines. Sans body text is held to a single quiet weight. Microcopy in mono uppercase, the tracking opened wide, behaves as field-notebook annotation — date, coordinate, taxonomic rank. The rhythm of the page is the rhythm of patient observation: a long heading, a short paragraph, a small technical aside, another rule. Repeat.

**Composition and balance.** Four numbered specimens form the spine of the page. They are equal in dignity, separated only by the gauge of a hairline. Nothing competes for attention; everything points to the next item. A single block of parchment fills the lower middle as the entry of "what the reader takes home." It is the only departure from paper, and it is intentional — the equivalent of a botanical illustrator's pressed-leaf insert.

**Visual hierarchy.** The reader's eye descends a single column, register by register: identity, intent, four specimens, deliverable, signature. There is no infographic flow chart. There is no decorative arrow. Hierarchy is established through **weight, size and the cadence of whitespace** — not through containers. This is the result of countless hours of refinement. Every alignment is a deliberate decision. Every space is measured. Every hairline is the work of a craftsman who has spent a career deciding when a line should appear and when silence will say more.

## Craftsmanship mandate

This piece must look as though it was set by an editor who specialises in scientific monographs, who has refused every Canva template ever offered to them, and who reviews every comma before going to press. Plain to the casual eye; revealing of master-level care to anyone who lingers. **Meticulously crafted. The product of deep expertise. Painstaking attention to the cadence of the grid.** Nothing here belongs to the visual idiom of a tech startup — and that, precisely, is the differentiator.
